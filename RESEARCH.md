# Research scope and editorial priorities

This document defines **what Pulsar researches**. The
[`research-and-write` skill](.agents/skills/research-and-write/SKILL.md) defines **how
research is verified, written, and published**. Review this policy before each
research run; do not infer a fixed topic list from article tags.

## Standing coverage areas

- **Science and health:** Significant peer-reviewed findings, major preprints
  with clear caveats, biomedical research, public health, and research policy.
- **Technology:** AI research and products, software engineering, computing
  infrastructure, cybersecurity, and technology policy.
- **Business and industry:** Material corporate developments, market structure,
  industrial strategy, and changes affecting products or operations.
- **Finance and markets:** Macroeconomic releases, monetary policy, financial
  markets, commodities, and evidence relevant to investment decisions.
- **Global affairs:** Geopolitics, international policy, conflicts, trade,
  energy security, and developments with cross-border consequences.

These are coverage areas, **not publication quotas or a ranked list**.
Choose topics based on evidence and significance rather than forcing an article
from every area in each run.

## Topic selection

1. Identify substantive developments or research questions in the standing
   coverage areas. For recurring news runs, look for material changes since
   previous coverage; for focused research, answer the specified question.
2. Prioritize topics with credible, inspectable evidence, meaningful novelty,
   potential scientific or practical impact, and relevance to decisions.
3. Distinguish a new finding or event from a repeat announcement, forecast
   restatement, preliminary claim, or already-covered story.
4. Favor original studies, official data and documents, and independently
   corroborated reporting. Apply the evidence and attribution requirements
   in the `research-and-write` skill.
5. Publish only what can be supported accurately. If nothing clears that bar,
   report the absence of a publishable finding; do not manufacture coverage.

## Exclusions and constraints

- Do not select a story solely because it is trending, promotional, or
  sensational. Exclude rumors and unsupported claims presented as fact.
- Do not treat preprints as peer-reviewed, vendor performance claims as
  independent benchmarks, or forecasts as observed outcomes.
- Do not repeat existing articles without substantive new evidence or a
  clearly identified correction or update.
- Do not publish investment or medical recommendations as established facts
  when the underlying evidence does not support them.
- Make uncertainty, limitations, conflicts of interest, and the distinction
  between observation and interpretation explicit in the article.

## Run-specific instructions

A user or scheduled run may specify a narrower focus, extra topics, geographic
scope, date range, or different order of priority. Apply those instructions
**for that run only**; do not silently change this standing policy. Explicit
run-specific topic requests may extend the coverage areas, but must still meet
the skill's evidence, sourcing, and verification standards.

For persistent editorial changes, update this file through version control.
The `tags` frontmatter is descriptive article metadata, not a configuration
mechanism for choosing research themes.
